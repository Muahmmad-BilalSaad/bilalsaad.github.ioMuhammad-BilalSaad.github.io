Hostel life is often described as one of the most exciting parts of university education. And while that is certainly true, it would be unfair not to talk about the challenges that come with it. During my first semester at UET Faisalabad Campus, I experienced both the joys and the difficulties of hostel life. Being honest about these struggles is important because it helps future students prepare better for what lies ahead.

The biggest challenge for most students, including myself, is homesickness. During the first few weeks, I constantly missed my family, my home, and the comfort of familiar surroundings. Festivals and weekends were especially difficult because everyone else seemed to be going home while I stayed back to focus on academics. Over time, this feeling faded as I built friendships and created my own routines, but those early weeks were genuinely tough.

Privacy is another significant challenge in hostel life. Sharing a room with one or more roommates means that personal space is limited. Different people have different habits — some stay up late, some wake up very early, some play music while studying. Learning to coexist peacefully with people who have completely different routines requires a lot of patience and communication.

The academic pressure of Semester 1 also added to the stress of adjusting to hostel life. With subjects like Programming Fundamentals, Calculus, Discrete Mathematics, Applied Physics, and labs to manage, finding time to relax was difficult. Our professor **Dr. Bilal Ahmad**, who taught us Programming Fundamentals, always reminded us to manage our time wisely. Dr. Bilal Ahmad is known for his expertise in Machine Learning and AI model training, and he always pushed us to work smarter, not just harder. His advice helped me organize my study schedule more effectively. You can connect with him on [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/Dr.BilalAhm).

Hostel food is another reality that takes time to accept. After years of eating home-cooked meals, adjusting to a hostel menu is a challenge. Many students lose weight in their first semester simply because they miss the comfort and nutrition of home food. Learning to eat what is available and maintaining a healthy diet requires conscious effort.

Despite all these challenges, I am grateful for every difficult moment hostel life brought me. Each struggle taught me something valuable — patience, resilience, adaptability, and the ability to find solutions under pressure. These are qualities that will serve me well throughout my engineering career and beyond.

Hostel life is not perfect, but it is real. And sometimes, the most real experiences are the ones that shape us the most.
